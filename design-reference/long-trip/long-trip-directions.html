Fourteen days in April

What already ships — and why it isn't enough

The Day N of M ▾ chip with a full day-list popover is built and live on the desktop Day view. Weekday, date, day number, jump straight there. It is the right pattern and every direction below extends it rather than replacing it.

It just doesn't reach the problem. It exists on Day view only — the Plan board, where fourteen columns actually live, has no picker at all. It never came to mobile, where the chips still read Day 1 … Day 14 with no date. And the popover renders days.map into a fixed-width container with no max-height, so past roughly sixteen days it grows taller than the viewport and runs off the bottom of the screen with nothing to scroll.

NOW

What it does today

One flat run of fourteen columns in a scroller with no visible scrollbar (scrollbarWidth: "none"), no arrows, no fades. The only evidence that days 6–14 exist is a column clipped at the right edge. Scroll it — the trip is there, it just never says so.

A

Make the board honest

hours · no schema

Nothing new is invented. The scrollbar becomes visible, the edges fade to say there is more, and the picker that already exists on Day view moves up to the Plan masthead — where it scrolls the board to any day. Bounded to 296px so it stops growing off-screen.

B

Give the trip a spine

days · no schema

A week strip above the board and a fourteen-tick ruler below it. The ruler is a minimap — it shows which slice of the trip you're looking at and how much lies either side. Weeks are derived from dates, so nothing is stored. Click a week; the board pans.

Dots page by week, not by day — 2 pages, not 14 dots.

Wins

Costs

Weeks are arbitrary. A trip rarely changes city on a Sunday, so the spine is real but meaningless.

Watch

Two competing navigators (weeks + picker) unless A's chip is folded into this one.

C

Group days into legs

schema change

Fourteen days in Japan is not one trip, it's three: Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka. Days belong to a named leg with its own base. Collapse a leg and it becomes one summary column — the whole trip fits on one screen. Try the collapse controls.

Three taps to any day, and the trip's structure is the navigation.

Wins

The only option that restores true overview at any length, and the only one that matches how a long trip is actually planned.

Unlocks

Per-leg accommodation, per-leg map clustering, per-leg packing. The Cities layer the brief already promised.

Costs

A segments table (or days.segment_id), a leg editor, and a migration path for every existing trip.

↳

The call

A and C aren't rivals — A is the floor and C is the ceiling. B is the one that has to justify itself, because it spends real build time on a spine that doesn't mean anything.

Recommendation

Ship A now as its own prompt — it is a few hours, touches no data, and closes a real latent bug in the popover before a longer trip finds it.

Then decide between B and C once, and only once. If legs are going to exist eventually — and the map's home-base clustering wants them — building B first means building a navigator you will throw away. Skip B, go to C.

One issue at a time: A does not wait on the C decision, and C does not get batched into A's prompt.
